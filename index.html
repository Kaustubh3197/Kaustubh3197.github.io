V2X CPM Perception & Visualization Pipeline
A ROS pipeline that parses radar data as ETSI ITS Collective Perception Messages, filters false detections, and renders the result live in RViz.
 + expand− collapse

Built as the perception stage of a four-part university V2X project. Radar data — either from a live perception-kit topic or recorded rosbag files — is parsed against roughly 130 custom ROS message definitions aligned to the ETSI ITS CPM standard, then filtered by confidence score and distance to reject phantom detections before being drawn as classified object markers (car, pedestrian) against an ego-vehicle model in RViz.

Left: unfiltered detections. Right: filtered output classified as cars and pedestrians.

02
Middleware Comparison for ADAS / SDV — Master's Thesis
Benchmarking OpenDDS, vsomeip, and Zenoh against an identical simulated ADAS workload to compare in-vehicle communication middleware.
 + expand− collapse

Eight in-vehicle communication protocols were first scored with a multi-criteria decision analysis across latency, determinism, QoS support, and ecosystem maturity. DDS, SOME/IP, and Zenoh came out ahead and were carried into full proof-of-concept implementations — the same seven-node ADAS scenario, data schema, and timing budgets reproduced identically on all three, so any performance differences trace back to the middleware itself rather than the application logic.

03
Gesture-Control & Obstacle-Avoiding Car
An Arduino build with two independent circuits — an accelerometer-and-RF433 gesture remote, and a standalone ultrasonic obstacle-avoidance mode.
 + expand− collapse

A hand-worn glove reads tilt from an accelerometer and encodes it over RF433 to a receiver on the car, which decodes the signal directly into a motor driver — no microcontroller needed on that side. A separate ultrasonic circuit sweeps a servo-mounted sensor to detect and steer around obstacles autonomously. Debugged and corrected two dead gesture states left over from the original build, where impossible numeric thresholds meant "backward" and "right" never registered.
